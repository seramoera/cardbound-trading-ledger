Weekly Increment Report

Week of: 24 September 2026

What changed this week

- Finished the initial Figma design for the browser application and used it to guide the front-end build.
- Built the onboarding screen, login flow, and account creation flow in the app so the first-time user journey is now in place.
- Updated the visual style to match the premium gold-on-dark direction, including the branding, buttons, spacing, and overall layout of the authentication screens.
- Set up the first version of the user sign-up and sign-in experience around the app’s intended flow, with a focus on making the app feel polished from the start.
- Kept the work honest and scoped: the onboarding, login, and create-account screens were completed in a meaningful way, while the rest of the app remained at placeholder level.

Why

The goal this week was to establish a clean and usable entry point for the product before building the rest of the application. A strong onboarding and authentication experience is essential because it sets the tone for the full app and makes the product feel complete from the first user interaction.

I also wanted to make sure the project matched the agreed design direction and that the user journey felt consistent with the app concept. It was more important to finish the initial flow properly than to rush ahead with unfinished screens that would not yet be user-ready.

What broke or what I got stuck on

- Some of the authentication setup was more difficult than expected because Supabase’s free plan limited how easily I could test email-based auth in practice.
- The original intent was to use email-based sign up and login, but this became impractical for testing within the current setup, so I shifted to a username and password flow for the initial build.
- The design work required several iterations to match the intended mockup closely, especially around spacing, button styling, and typography.
- Some of the visual refinements were time-consuming because the UI needed to feel polished and consistent rather than simply functional.

What is left

- Finish the remaining screens beyond onboarding, login, and account creation.
- Build the core app flows for the main product experience rather than leaving them as placeholders.
- Complete the rest of the user journey and ensure the app is functional end-to-end.
- Continue integrating the backend and data flows so the app moves from a polished entry experience into a full working product.

Week of: 27 September 2026

What changed this week

- Continued refining the app to feel more like a finished product rather than a prototype.
  
Why

The goal for this stage was to move past the onboarding flow and begin making the application feel like a real trading dashboard. The home screen and partner form are core parts of the user journey and help establish how the product will function in practice.

What broke or what I got stuck on

- The form and dashboard styling needed several design iterations to match the mockup more closely.
- Small differences in spacing, icon treatment, and typography were time-consuming because the app relies heavily on a very specific visual system.
- Some layout details had to be adjusted repeatedly to preserve consistency across screens without overloading the interface.

What is left

- Continue building the main trading workflow beyond the dashboard and partner form.
- Connect the form and dashboard UI to working data flows.
- Finish the rest of the app screens and keep refining the visual consistency across the full product.
